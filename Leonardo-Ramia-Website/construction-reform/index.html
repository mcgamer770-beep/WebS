Construction Reform

Building a culture of reuse.

A decarbonisation institute that questions unnecessary construction. The proposal brings material intake, sorting, storage and redistribution together with research and public learning. Its organisation makes the processes of reuse visible, connecting advocacy with the practical work of reform.

Mount Tomah, NSW · 2026Institution / Material reuse

01CONCEPT & PROGRAMME

Questioning the need to build.

Construction Reform proposes an institute organised around four connected activities: Advocate, Enable, Circulate and Reform. Public learning sits alongside material handling and research into alternatives.

02DESIGN DEVELOPMENT

From material cycle to spatial order.

The design develops from a conceptual reading of decarbonisation into an operational sequence: intake, sort, store and dispatch. Further studies test how that organisation can respond to different sites.

03PLANS & SECTIONS

A connected institute in the landscape.

Drafted drawings develop the proposal’s spatial organisation and relationship to changing ground levels. Collage studies then explore the occupation and atmosphere of the same spaces.

04MATERIAL STRATEGY

Reuse as a design decision.

The proposed material hierarchy prioritises retention and reuse, followed by recycling and repurposing. New material is introduced only where necessary. The assembly study explores salvaged structure and alternative materials.

05SUPPORTING INVESTIGATIONS

Testing the wider argument.

These related studies support the Studio 4 investigation. They are presented separately from the Mount Tomah proposal.
